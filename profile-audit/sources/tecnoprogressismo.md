# Tecnoprogressismo / Techno-Progressivism

## Scope and sources

This profile represents the democratic, egalitarian technoprogressive current articulated by the Institute for Ethics and Emerging Technologies (IEET), especially the 2014 Technoprogressive Declaration. It is a movement-level interpretation, not a profile of any individual author, party or national government.

Sources consulted on 2026-10-10:

1. [IEET, Philosophy](https://ieet.org/philosophy/): institutional statement linking technological development with democratic social change, technology access and human rights. Supports the combination of innovation and public accountability.
2. [Mike Treder, Toward a Technoprogressive Manifesto (2009)](https://ieet.org/archive/articles/treder20090530.html): connects democratic liberalism, safe and accessible enhancement, international human rights and cooperation, and environmental sustainability. Economic regulation should not be mistaken for police authoritarianism; technological enthusiasm should not be mistaken for indifference to safety.
3. [James Hughes, The Technoprogressive Agenda After Fascism (2025), including the 2014 Declaration](https://ieet.substack.com/p/the-technoprogressive-agenda-after): the declaration supports democracy, self-determination, access to enhancement, social guarantees and transnational risk reduction. The 2025 essay illustrates public research, AI governance and redistributive policy. Its specific instruments are the author's proposals, not unanimous positions of every technoprogressive.
4. [James Hughes, Problems of Transhumanism: Introduction (2010)](https://ieet.org/archive/articles/hughes20100105.html): situates the current in Enlightenment traditions of reason, technological progress, democracy and equality, including Condorcet as an antecedent.
5. [Wikipedia, Techno-progressivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techno-progressivism): secondary orientation and bibliographic cross-check, rather than the sole basis for assigning answers.

## Distinction from existing entries

- **Technocracy / Left-Wing Technocracy:** expertise informs policy, but does not replace democratic accountability with rule by unelected technical committees.
- **Effective Accelerationism:** innovation is paired with risk governance and equitable access rather than an unconditional commitment to market acceleration.
- **Left Accelerationism / Techno-Socialism:** some technoprogressives support post-capitalist proposals, but abolition of all markets or revolutionary party rule is not a requirement of this profile.
- **Social Democracy / Progressivism:** overlap is expected. The distinctive commitments concern bodily and cognitive self-determination, enhancement, longevity, automation, and access to emerging technologies. The twelve aggregate axes may capture these distinctions only imperfectly.

## Interpretation limits

The 240 answers and five archetype choices are an independent AI simulation of the doctrine, not statements collected from members. Every answer was checked against the current `questions-pool.json` text, and archetypes were generated from `profile_vector.py --prompt-block`. Source support is strongest for democracy, civil and bodily liberties, social provision, and technological development. Territorial administration, precise trade instruments and military policy require more interpretation. Neither cosmopolitan cooperation nor the existence of a national state implies aggressive nationalist foreign policy.

The audit must not be adjusted to obtain a desired vector, ranking, category, or distance from another profile. Similarity warnings are reviewed as limitations of this representation, not optimized away.
